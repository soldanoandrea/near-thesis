NEAR on the evidence: verified Intents revenue, thin organic use, and an 8.1x gap to cover dilution

A data-backed long-term thesis on NEAR for the NEAR HACKATHON 2026, track thesis. Snapshot as-of 2026-10-29 (UTC). Every number in this essay is filled in by make site from the tables in data/processed/; NUMBERS.md names the table and row behind each one. Charts: the dashboard. Written by an AI agent team with no NEAR position. Not financial advice.

Verdict

On the evidence at 2026-10-29, NEAR is two businesses of very different quality. The chain's own activity is mostly reward programs, bots and relays: organic, human-like signers sent 2.17% of all transactions in 2026-Q3 (90% interval 0.86% to 3.70%), and organic daily active signers averaged 6,437 (1,935 to 12,045), against a point estimate of 23,402 in 2025-Q1 that rests on 2 organic draws. Since 2025-Q3 every quarter has stayed between 6,437 and 9,633, so organic use is small and not growing; the fitted trend of -37.83% a year leans on early 2025 and forecasts only slightly better than a random walk. NEAR Intents, the cross-chain swap layer that settles on NEAR, is real, and its captured revenue checks out: rebuilt from public wallet flows it is 98.68% of what DefiLlama reports, and since 2026-04 99.7% of it has come straight from the Intents contract. Its reported volume does not check out one to one: public on-chain swaps, each counted once, add up to 46.04% of DefiLlama's figure, so the headline volume either counts both sides of each swap or includes flow that public events cannot see. Value accrual does not yet cover issuance: after burn and buybacks the float grows 2.12% a year at the current 2.5% cap, and offsetting that would take 8.1x today's Intents revenue (5.1x if HSP-029 cuts issuance to 1.6%). Our long-term case is therefore conditional, not bullish: it needs Intents revenue to grow several-fold while organic use stops shrinking. Under our stated priors the probability that burn plus buybacks cover issuance within 5 years is 0.30; that is simply the weight we gave the +60%-a-year revenue path, the only one that gets there, not a measurement. The 7 dated predictions at the end, hashed and tagged before their outcomes exist, say when we will find out.

How to read this

  • The questions are the seven of PIANO.md, written before the data were fetched: Q1 is the activity real, Q2 is NEAR Intents volume real, Q3 does the AI-agent story show up on-chain, Q4 supply and value accrual, Q5 fees and take rate, Q6 developers, Q7 comparables.
  • Evidence grades: A means re-runnable from chain data (archival RPC, the FastNear indexer); B means an aggregator snapshot (DefiLlama, NearBlocks, CoinGecko, Electric Capital). Where we sampled, intervals are 90% bootstrap intervals over sampled blocks.
  • A falsifier that is met counts against the bullish case. The full scorecard is on the dashboard.
  • Priors were written down before the per-signer and Intents data were analysed (priors.md; the git history shows the order). We quote them where the data moved them.

Method

Data. Only keyless public APIs: NearBlocks, DefiLlama, CoinGecko, NEAR RPC and archival RPC, the FastNear transactions indexer, Blockscout for Aurora, GitHub, Electric Capital Open Dev Data and the NEAR governance forum. 50 raw files (307,872 rows) were fetched between 2026-10-30T19:47:20Z and 2026-10-31T01:57:54Z and committed with their URL, parameters, fetch time and sha256 in data/raw/MANIFEST.json. make reproduce rebuilds every processed table, report, this essay and the dashboard offline, and the tests check that the committed outputs are byte-identical to what the code produces.

Sampling. For activity we draw 30 uniform random blocks per month since 2020 (90 since 2025-11) and keep every transaction in them. From those we draw 200 transactions per month since 2024-11 (15 before), seeded, and for each draw we read the real signer's full month from the FastNear indexer: its exact transaction count, up to 200 timestamps, and for subsets its first appearance and its latest receivers and methods. For relayed meta-transactions the real signer is the inner signer, not the relayer. Distinct active signers are estimated with 1/c weights (a signer with c transactions is c times as likely to be drawn). The method was validated against exact counts: the census share of the largest app, HOT, falls inside the sample's 90% interval in 93.94% of months.

Bot classification. A rule-based score on five features (volume, round-the-clock activity, sub-30-second median gaps, metronome-like regularity, one repeated action) labels a signer automated at a score of 2 or more. Signers mostly calling reward programs (HOT, KaiChing, SWEAT, NEARCrowd, inscriptions and their namespaces) are incentivized; automated signers calling settlement, bridge, MPC or EVM-relay contracts are infrastructure; fresh implicit accounts calling themselves are account setup; the rest is organic. The score is not trained on labelled data. Moving the threshold from strict to lenient moves the 2026-Q3 organic share from 1.17% to 5.17%.

Reconciliation. Four independent checks against reported numbers:

  • NEAR Intents volume: on 6 sampled days we counted every fee-paying settlement transaction (the fee collector appears in the event logs of exactly those transactions), rebuilt a cluster sample of 4,998 of them from DIP-4 token_diff events, netted each account's legs, counted each swap once and priced it with DefiLlama's historical prices. A control sample of every intents.near transaction in short windows of each day found 0 swaps that escaped the fee-paying frame.
  • Captured revenue: DefiLlama's own rule (NEAR and wNEAR received by the three revenue wallets from outside the set) applied to the complete on-chain histories of those wallets.
  • Treasury: archival month-start balances of treasury.near minus its transaction flows recover the protocol rewards it receives without a receipt; transfers of 100,000 NEAR or more are followed for up to five hops.
  • Supply: the per-block identity supply(b) = supply(b-1) - burnt(b) holds exactly, in yoctoNEAR, in 100.00% of sampled block pairs; issuance is measured from supply changes at epoch boundaries.

Models. Four pure functions with unit tests: a monthly supply recursion under the 2.5% cap and under HSP-029; fee and revenue multiples against Solana, Sui, Aptos, Ethereum and Tron; adoption models (log-linear, logistic, random walk) scored by rolling-origin backtests; and staking real yields. Their inputs, assumptions and ranges are printed in models_summary.md.

What could not be measured. Confidential intents, and any swap that does not pay the protocol fee, are invisible on-chain by design. DefiLlama's Intents volume comes from a NEAR-uploaded Dune table whose definition is not published. No account carries an entity label, so implicit accounts in the treasury trail are unattributed. Only 1 new-signer cohort has enough draws to measure retention. The lead-lag test of the starter hypothesis (does Intents volume lead usage better than price?) was not run. Agent activity is measured only through the Chain Signatures proxy. The HSP-029 vote had not closed at the snapshot. BigQuery, Dune and the Intents Explorer were not used because they need accounts or keys.

The evidence, question by question

Q1. Is the activity real?

Headline activity has collapsed, and most of what remains is not organic use. NearBlocks counts 745,246 transactions a day over the last 30 days, down 83.64% on a year earlier. In 2026-Q3 the transactions split by signer class as follows (90% intervals in the chart):

ClassShare of transactions, 2026-Q390% interval

Automated (bots)21.83%18.65% to 25.16%
Incentivized programs55.83%51.48% to 59.55%
Infrastructure relays19.33%16.39% to 22.69%
Account setup0.83%0.32% to 1.53%

Our prior, recorded before the signer data were analysed, was an organic share of about 25%. The data put it an order of magnitude lower: 16,630 organic transactions a day out of 739,248. Most of the "untracked" traffic that looked organic in aggregate turned out, signer by signer, to be automated accounts or reward programs we had not listed.

Counting accounts gives a second surprise. All real signers averaged 149,876 a day in 2026-Q3 (130,310 to 171,084), which is 1.9x what NearBlocks reports as active accounts in the last six complete months (2026-04 to 2026-09; the 12-month median is 5.2x, because reward-program traffic was much larger before 2026-04). The gap has the opposite sign to our prior: relayed meta-transactions are signed on the outside by a relayer, and NearBlocks appears to count the relayer, not the user. Its "active accounts" undercount accounts and say nothing about their quality: most of the missing signers are reward-program accounts, not organic users.

Organic daily active signers were 6,437 in 2026-Q3 against 23,402 in 2025-Q1, the first quarter with the full sample design. The point estimates fell 72.49%, but the 2025-Q1 interval (0 to 55,725) reaches zero, so the sample does not resolve the size of the decline: the 2025-Q1 estimate rests on 2 organic draws out of 600 (2026-Q3: 13). From 2025-Q3 on, the quarterly estimates stay between 6,437 and 9,633 with overlapping intervals, so most of the fitted decline comes from early 2025. The log-linear trend over 2025-01 to 2026-09 is -37.83% a year. Out of sample it barely beats a random walk: mean absolute log error 0.404 against 0.422 at six months (2 forecasts) and 0.315 against 0.363 at three months (5 forecasts). We read it as a description, not a forecast; extrapolated, it puts organic daily active signers at 4,117 in a year (1,352 to 10,671). Retention cannot carry the case either way: the one informative cohort (2025-Q1) kept 57.73% of its new signers active three months later (33.35% to 78.83%).

Q2. Is NEAR Intents volume what it is reported to be?

DefiLlama reports $32.79B of NEAR Intents volume over the last 365 days, $9.79B in 2026-Q3 alone, up 56.68% on the previous quarter after a change of -13.04% in 2026-Q2. Our on-chain rebuild on 6 sampled days finds public swaps, each counted once, worth 46.04% of DefiLlama's daily figure at the median (from 22.86% to 64.76% by day). Counting both legs of every swap gives 92.08%, and 100% sits inside the 90% interval on 4 of the 6 days; on the other 2 even the two-sided equivalent stays below DefiLlama's figure. Since no swap escaped our frame, two readings remain, and they can both be true: DefiLlama's volume counts roughly both sides of each swap, or about half of the volume settles where public events cannot see it (confidential intents). Either way the swaps are real and settle on NEAR, and the reported series is best read as an upper bound. Our prior gave 0.45 to a ratio below 0.8; that branch happened.

Volume is concentrated: the largest asset, USDT, is 47.93% of sold volume at the median of sampled days, above the 40% fragility line we set in PIANO.md.

Q3. Does the AI-agent story show up on-chain?

Only through a proxy. Transactions that reach Chain Signatures (v1.signer), the MPC signing service that lets NEAR accounts and agents sign on other chains, were 6.64% of all transactions in 2026-Q3 and 1,304,107 in 2026-09. That clears the falsifier's 1% line, but we did not attribute any of it to AI agents. The agent narrative is not measured here, so it carries no weight in this thesis.

Q4. Supply, value accrual and the treasury

Issuance. The cut of the inflation cap from 5% to 2.5% in late October 2025 is visible in the data: realized supply growth fell from 5.01% to 2.48% over consecutive 365-day windows, and sampled epochs show issuance of 2.49% a year. Fee burn offsets only 1.63% of what is minted. 45.30% of the supply is staked.

Revenue and buybacks. Captured Intents revenue rebuilt on-chain is 98.68% of DefiLlama's figure over the last six months, and between 90.17% and 111.10% in each of the 12 months with revenue (chart). It is the most trustworthy Intents number we have, with one caveat on what it verifies: the check applies DefiLlama's own rule (any NEAR the revenue wallets receive from outside the set) to the same wallets, so it confirms the arithmetic and the source accounts, not that every inflow was a swap fee. From 2025-11 to 2026-03 only 19% of the captured value came from intents.near, and in 4 of those months most of it came from one account, auroraliqsupport.near (see data/raw/fastnear_tx/wallet_flows.csv), which chain data do not label. Since 2026-04, 99.7% has come straight from intents.near. Since buybacks started on 2026-02-23 the revenue wallets captured 3.11M NEAR; the buyback wallet sent only 12,025 NEAR onward. The NEAR is bought back and held, not burned, so it shrinks the float, not the supply. At the last six months' pace that is 4.59M NEAR a year, or 0.35% of supply.

Dilution. Net of burn and buybacks the float grows 2.12% a year at the 2.5% cap. A staker's share of the float grows 2.51% a year (5% commission) while a non-staker's shrinks 2.06%. For burn plus buybacks to offset issuance at today's price and take rate, captured revenue would have to reach $151.73M a year, against $18.80M today: 8.1x. HSP-029, which phases the cap down to 1.6% over 24 months, lowers the bar to $96.20M (5.1x); it was in status "Voting" at our snapshot (2026-10-30T22:59:35Z), with the vote reported to close on 2026-11-02 (chart).

The treasury. treasury.near receives its share of protocol rewards as the protocol says it should: 100.02% of the expected amount (median of the last six months). Its outflows are another matter. It sent out 30.17M NEAR in April and May 2026, almost emptying it (Sankey):

  • In April, 9.80M NEAR went to two ecosystem multisigs, then through one implicit account to opgran01.near and on to nfendowment03.near. Amounts grow along the way (nfendowment03.near received 21.43M NEAR from opgran01.near) because these accounts hold other funds; nfendowment03.near then sent 29.15M NEAR to 22 accounts, most of them unlabelled.
  • In May, 20.37M NEAR went to one implicit account and on through three or four more implicit accounts to the staking pools of p2p-org (10.77M NEAR) and figment (10.19M NEAR): 20.95M NEAR into staking in total.

Within five hops no transfer of 100,000 NEAR or more reached an exchange-like account or NEAR Intents (0 such stops). The rest ends at unlabelled implicit accounts, the largest of which received 64.17M NEAR beyond the fifth hop. NEAR is fungible, so these are amounts that moved after each receipt, not the same coins; the names are account ids, not verified entities.

Q5. Fees and the take rate

The chain itself earns almost nothing: $202.4k of fees in the last 30 days and $1.21M over a year, 0.04% of market cap annualized. NEAR Intents is where the fees are: $69.10M a year annualized from the last 90 days, of which NEAR captures $18.80M (27.20%); the rest never reaches NEAR's revenue wallets (DefiLlama's gross fees include the affiliate fees paid to integrators and front-ends). The captured take rate rose from 2.40 bp of volume in 2026-Q2 to 3.13 bp in 2026-Q3, when revenue grew 104.81% against volume growth of 56.68%. The falsifier "fees competed away" is not met.

Q6. Developers

Electric Capital counts 563 monthly active NEAR developers in 2026-09, down 36.24% on a year earlier, and NEAR's share of all web3 developers fell from 2.19% to 1.87%. But the peer chains lost developers faster: NEAR's count relative to Solana, Sui, Aptos, Base, Ethereum and Tron combined rose from 3.07% to 4.19% between 2025-Q3 and 2026-Q3. Core development continues: nearcore commits changed 4.95% year on year, from 60 authors.

The bull case

Each claim names the prediction that will test it (falsifiers.md, frozen in predictions.csv).

  1. NEAR Intents is a real, growing and verifiable revenue line. Captured revenue reconciles with on-chain flows (98.68%), it doubled in 2026-Q3 (up 104.81% on the quarter), and the take rate rose. Tested by P2 (90-day volume to 2027-04-30 at least $11.30B), P4 (captured revenue rebuilt on-chain for 2026-11 to 2027-04 at least $4.46M, checked 2027-04-30) and P5 (take rate at least 3.28 bp on 2027-04-30).
  2. Counting Intents, NEAR is cheap against its peers. 80.0x fees against a peer median of 379x, and 220x captured revenue against 1,784x. Tested by P7: the multiple stays at or below the peer median (today 379x) on 2027-04-30.
  3. Supply discipline is real and may tighten. Issuance halved in the data, buybacks take about 0.35% of supply a year off the market into a held balance, and HSP-029 would lower the offset bar to 5.1x. First check: the HSP-029 vote, reported to close on 2026-11-02; then the structural Q4 falsifier (float dilution above 2% a year), re-measured by every make refresh.
  4. NEAR's developer base is holding up relative to its peers (3.07% to 4.19%). Tested by P6: at least 3.38% of the peer-chain developer count in 2027-Q3, checked 2027-10-31.
  5. The chain-abstraction rail is used. Chain Signatures carries 6.64% of transactions. Tested by P3: at least 1,304,107 transactions in September 2027, checked 2027-10-31.
  6. The money is traceable. Treasury rewards reconcile to 100.02%, revenue to 98.68%, and 20.95M NEAR of the 2026 treasury outflows went into staking.

The bear case

The same weight, the same tests: each claim names the prediction whose failure would confirm it.

  1. Organic usage is small and not growing. 2.17% of transactions, 6,437 organic daily active signers, between 6,437 and 9,633 in every quarter since 2025-Q3. Confirmed if P1 fails: organic daily active signers in 2027-Q3 below 1,935 (the lower end of today's interval), checked 2027-10-31. P1 is lenient by design; a pass near the threshold would still be a decline.
  2. The headline metrics overstate the economy. Incentivized programs and bots sent 55.83% and 21.83% of 2026-Q3 transactions; NearBlocks' active accounts miss real signers by 1.9x (last six months); DefiLlama's Intents volume is about twice the public one-sided volume. No prediction tests a definition; the Q2 falsifier on reconstruction is already met.
  3. Intents is concentrated. One asset, USDT, makes up 47.93% of sold volume. A stablecoin-route shock would hit P2 and P4 together.
  4. Holders are diluted and will stay so without large revenue growth. Float dilution 2.12% a year, non-staker real yield -2.06%, offset needs 8.1x today's revenue. Confirmed if P4 fails (captured revenue below $4.46M for 2026-11 to 2027-04).
  5. On its own fees the chain is priced on narrative. 2,516x annualized chain fees against a peer median of 379x, and NEAR captures only 27.20% of Intents fees. Confirmed if P7 fails at 2027-04-30.
  6. Developers are leaving in absolute terms: down 36.24% in a year. Confirmed if P6 fails at 2027-10-31.
  7. The treasury's 2026 outflows are unattributed. 30.17M NEAR left treasury.near in two months, much of it to accounts with no public label. No prediction covers it; a governance disclosure of the recipients would settle it.

Valuation: ranges and priors, not a price target

We do not give a price target. Three lenses, each with its range.

Relative value. At the peer median multiple, NEAR's market cap would be $931.94M on chain fees alone and $50.30B on captured revenue including Intents, against $6.20B at the snapshot. A range that wide says the multiple lens is indeterminate until one decides how much of Intents belongs to NEAR holders. The data verify that the captured part exists; they do not show that the market should capitalize it at a peer multiple.

What you have to believe. For holders to stop being diluted at today's price, captured Intents revenue has to reach $151.73M a year (8.1x today) at the 2.5% cap, or $96.20M (5.1x) under HSP-029. A higher NEAR price raises the bar in dollars, because buybacks buy fewer tokens.

Scenarios with explicit priors. Model (a) projects float growth over five years for three policies and three paths of Intents revenue in NEAR terms, from 2.34% a year (2.5% cap, revenue changing -30% a year) to 0.29% a year (HSP-029 active after three months, revenue changing +60% a year). Our weights, recorded in valuation_priors.toml and quoted here as opinions:

These weights are the least evidence-based numbers in this document; change them in the file and make site recomputes the result.

P1 (the bottom of today's interval), P6 (the lowest of the last four quarters), P4 ($4.46M in six months, while captured revenue ran at $18.80M a year over the last 90 days) and P7 (80.0x today against a ceiling of 379x) leave wide margins: their passing would be weak evidence for the bull case, their failing strong evidence against it.

Decision rule, stated now: if P1, P2 and P4 all hold, we upgrade the case from conditional to supported on usage and value capture; if P1 and P4 both fail, the long-term case as stated here is falsified. Before those dates, three things would move us: the HSP-029 result (a pass lowers the offset bar from 8.1x to 5.1x); a published definition of DefiLlama's Intents volume (it would tell us whether the missing half is double counting or confidential flow); and a public account of the April-May treasury transfers.

Limitations and known biases

  • Small samples for rare events. Organic draws are a few per month, so organic intervals are wide and one month can move a quarter. Monthly active signer estimates are noisier still; we lean on daily ones.
  • A rule-based classifier. No labelled data exists for NEAR signers. A human using a batching app can look like a bot, and a slow bot can look human. The sensitivity range (1.17% to 5.17%) is the honest error bar on the label, beyond the sampling interval.
  • Labels refined after a first look. The list of reward programs was extended after we inspected the receivers of draws labelled organic in pilot months (PlayEmber, Harvest Moon, HOT claim namespaces; see config/sources.toml). Each addition moved draws out of organic, so the organic share depends on those choices; the share under the original list is not reported.
  • Asymmetric multiples. The Intents-inclusive multiples add the fees of NEAR's largest application to NEAR, but no application fees to the peers, whose chain fees exclude their DEX and app fees. That flatters NEAR; the captured-revenue basis (220x against 1,784x) is the fairer comparison.
  • Changes from the plan. PIANO.md planned prediction thresholds from the models' 80% intervals, a cross-sectional valuation regression (Q7) and lead-lag tests (Q2); the thresholds instead use today's values, interval bounds and recent minima, and the regression and the lead-lag tests were not run.
  • Single-sourced aggregates. Intents volume and gross fees come from a NEAR-uploaded dataset via DefiLlama; we verified captured revenue, not gross fees. Market caps are CoinGecko's, and the keyless API gives only 365 days.
  • One snapshot. Every number is as of 2026-10-29. A refresh can move them, and the peer set (five chains with a token) is our choice.
  • Simple models. Constant price, no feedback from price to usage, no regime changes beyond the ones listed. Model forecasts are extrapolations and are labelled as such.
  • Priors partly reconstructed. The Q1 and Q2 priors were committed before those data were analysed; the Q4, Q6 and model priors were written after those results were seen and are marked as reconstructions in priors.md.
  • Incentives. This was written for a NEAR-sponsored hackathon, a setting that rewards good news about NEAR. To counter that pull we gave the bear case the same space and the same tests, pre-registered the falsifiers in PIANO.md, and hashed the predictions before their outcomes exist.
  • Who wrote it. An AI agent team (Claude models in Claude Code) with no NEAR position, directed by one human. AI-written code can be wrong in confident ways: every number here is computed by tested code from committed data and filled into this text by the build, so a number cannot drift from the pipeline without a test failing.

Reproduce it

Two commands on macOS or Linux with make and uv installed; no account, no key, and no network access beyond uv installing Python 3.12 and the locked packages:

git clone https://github.com/soldanoandrea/near-thesis && cd near-thesis
make reproduce

make reproduce rebuilds data/processed/, the three reports, this essay and the dashboard from the committed raw snapshots, then runs the tests; git status shows nothing modified afterwards. Open site/index.html for this essay and site/dashboard.html for the charts. make refresh re-downloads every snapshot from the public APIs (several hours, rate limited) when you want to check a prediction.
